HW1 scored 96/100. Both 2-point deductions were on references (not physics). Two rules to follow on every SPCE 5065 submission:

1. **Number references in the order they are first cited in the text (AIAA convention).** On HW1 the first inline citation in the body was [2], and the professor marked: "-2, put references in sequential order. This should be [1]." Do NOT number by importance or theme (e.g., do not make the lecture [1] just because it is the main source). The first source cited in the body is [1], the next new source is [2], and so on. After drafting, walk the body top to bottom and renumber so the [n] markers ascend 1, 2, 3 with no out-of-order jumps, then reorder the Sources list to match.

2. **Cite every external/published value inline at the point of use.** The professor wrote "Reference? -2" on Problem 7, where the published Saturn mass was stated without an inline citation. Having it in the Sources list is not enough; the [n] must appear right where the value is used. This one was self-inflicted: the original draft read "the NASA Saturn Fact Sheet gives ... [9]" and that inline cite got dropped when the section was rewritten for the hand-calc version. NEVER drop an inline citation when condensing or rewriting prose.

Positive note: the professor wrote "Nice graph!" on the Figure 1 velocity plot, so the matplotlib figures land well. Keep producing them.

**Why:** Direct grader feedback on HW1 (96/100). Both deductions were avoidable citation-formatting issues. Related: [[feedback_submission_voice]], [[feedback_no_em_dashes]].

**How to apply:** Before finalizing any submission, do a dedicated references pass: (a) renumber [n] markers by first appearance in the text, (b) confirm every external fact, number, or quote has an inline [n] at its location, (c) confirm the Sources list order matches the new numbering.
