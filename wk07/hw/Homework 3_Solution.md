- 2. What is meant by “altered vestibular functions” and what are its symptoms?

Altered vestibular functions refers to changes in the body's balance and spatial orientation system caused by the absence of gravity. On Earth, the vestibular organs in the inner ear use gravity to help determine which way is up and to coordinate balance, posture, and eye movements. In microgravity, these organs receive conflicting sensory information, leading to symptoms collectively known as Space Adaptation Syndrome. Common symptoms include dizziness, nausea, motion sickness, disorientation, vertigo, headaches, impaired balance, and difficulty coordinating movements. Most astronauts adapt within a few days, but these effects can temporarily reduce performance during critical mission tasks.

- 6. Describe the major breakdowns and disconnects that occurred during the Apollo 13 mission. Which elements of the SHELL model were involved?


Answers will vary, but here is one example: The Apollo 13 accident resulted from a combination of technical failures and human-system disconnects rather than a single mistake. The immediate cause was an oxygen tank explosion caused by damaged wiring inside the tank, but underlying causes included design flaws, inadequate testing after modifications, and communication breakdowns between engineering teams. Once the explosion occurred, the crew lost electrical power, oxygen, and much of the command module's functionality, forcing them to rely on the lunar module as a lifeboat. Throughout the emergency, successful recovery depended on effective communication and coordination between the astronauts, Mission Control, engineers, and contractors. Within the SHELL model, several interfaces were involved:

- • Liveware–Hardware (L-H): The oxygen tank design, damaged wiring, thermostat incompatibility, and failure of critical spacecraft hardware were the primary technical failures.
- • Liveware–Software (L-S): Testing procedures, operational documentation, engineering analyses, and mission procedures failed to identify the hazards associated with the modified oxygen tank.


- • Liveware–Environment (L-E): The space environment left the crew with no opportunity for repair or rescue, while limited power, oxygen, and thermal control greatly increased mission risk.
- • Liveware–Liveware (L-L): Effective communication among the crew, Mission Control, and engineering teams became the key factor that enabled the safe return of the astronauts despite the equipment failures.


Apollo 13 demonstrates that successful human spaceflight depends not only on reliable hardware, but also on well-designed procedures, effective communication, and the ability of humans to adapt when unexpected failures occur.
